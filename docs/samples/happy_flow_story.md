# The Silver Seal

## Chapter 1: The Valley

The valley of Millbrook lay between two grey ridges, and for three generations it had belonged to the Harlan family. Old John Harlan, a broad, quiet farmer with hands like roots, rose before dawn every day to walk his barley fields. His wife, Anna Harlan, kept the farmhouse and the ledgers, and it was Anna who held the family together when the harvests failed.

Their only son was also named John Harlan. Everyone in Millbrook called him Johnny, because two Johns under one roof had caused confusion since the boy was born. Johnny was nineteen, tall like his father but restless, and he hated the plough. He spent his evenings in the village tavern, the Gilded Goose, where the innkeeper's daughter, Mira Castell, served ale and laughed at his stories of the cities beyond the ridges.

"You will inherit this land, Johnny," Old John said one evening, standing at the edge of the barley. "A Harlan does not run from the soil."

"A Harlan does not have to be buried in it either," Johnny answered, and went back toward the village without waiting for a reply.

That night Old John climbed to the attic and took down a small iron chest that had been in the family for generations. Inside lay a silver seal, a heavy ring engraved with a hawk holding a key. He turned it in the lamplight for a long while. He had never told Anna or Johnny what it was. He only knew that his own father had made him swear never to sell it and never to wear it in the sight of strangers.

## Chapter 2: The Stranger

On the first day of autumn a stranger rode into Millbrook. He wore a grey cloak with the hood drawn low, and he rode a black horse that had clearly travelled far. He took a room at the Gilded Goose and paid in old coin. When Mira Castell asked his name, he said only that people called him the Pilgrim.

The Pilgrim asked questions. He asked about the Harlan farm, about the old mill on the river, and about whether any family in the valley owned heirlooms from before the war. Mira told Johnny about it the next evening, and Johnny, who was bored and curious, followed the Pilgrim to the old mill. There he found the stranger standing alone among the broken wheels, looking at the water.

"You are the Harlan boy," the Pilgrim said without turning. "Which one are you? The father or the son?"

"The son. Johnny Harlan. My father is Old John, and he has no heirlooms, if that is what you are after."

"Everyone has something," the Pilgrim said quietly. "Tell your father that an old friend of his father's house is in the valley. Tell him I mean no harm."

Johnny did not tell his father. Instead he told Mira, and Mira, who trusted nobody who hid his face, told her father, Tobias Castell, the innkeeper. By morning half of Millbrook was whispering about the Pilgrim in the grey cloak.

## Chapter 3: Twenty Years Earlier

Twenty years before the Pilgrim came to Millbrook, the valley had been a different place. The Kingdom of Ravenscar was at war with its own northern lords, and Old John Harlan, then a young man of twenty-four, had marched with the king's army as a quartermaster. He was not a fighter, but he was honest, and honesty was rare in that army.

At the siege of Ravenscar Keep, a wounded nobleman named Lord Edric Vane was carried into John's supply tent. Lord Edric was the king's cousin and the last heir of a ruined house, and the northern lords had put a price on his head. For three nights John hid him among the grain sacks and tended his wounds. When the keep fell, Lord Edric pressed a silver seal into John's hand. It bore a hawk holding a key, the crest of the house of Vane.

"Keep this safe," Lord Edric whispered. "Never sell it, and never wear it where my enemies can see. One day I will come back for it, and for the debt I owe you."

John carried the seal home to Millbrook, hid it in an iron chest, and told no one. A year later he married Anna, and a year after that their son was born. The war ended, the king died, and Lord Edric Vane vanished. Most people believed he had been killed. John wanted to believe it too, because a debt from a man like that was a dangerous thing to own.

## Chapter 4: The Hood

The Pilgrim came to the Harlan farm at dusk, and Old John met him at the gate with a pitchfork in his hands. Anna stood behind him in the doorway, and Johnny stood beside the barn with Mira Castell, who had followed him up the valley road.

"Put it down, John Harlan," the Pilgrim said. "I have waited twenty years to say thank you."

He pushed back his hood. His face was lined and pale, with a long scar across the left cheek, but John knew it at once. The Pilgrim was Lord Edric Vane.

"You are alive," John said, and the pitchfork slipped from his fingers.

"Barely, and not for long, unless I reclaim what I left with you. The northern lords are dead, but their sons are not. The Silver Seal proves my family's claim to the northern lands. Without it I am only a scarred old man. With it, I am the rightful Lord of Ravenscar."

Old John went into the house and returned with the iron chest. Anna gasped when she saw the ring, and Johnny stared at his father as if seeing him for the first time. Old John placed the Silver Seal in Lord Edric's palm.

"Your father hid a lord in a grain tent, Johnny," Lord Edric said, closing his fist around the ring. "That is what the Harlans do. They do not run from the soil, and they do not run from their word."

Johnny looked at the barley fields, then at Mira, and for the first time he did not want to leave the valley. The next morning Lord Edric Vane rode north with the Silver Seal on his finger, and Johnny Harlan rode beside him as his squire.
